Disaster recovery

Backups and recovery after a disaster.

Purpose

TThe purpose of the Disaster Recovery Plan is to ensure continuity of African Foods Company’s key business operations should there be any IT system failure, natural disaster, or major lockdown. African Foods Company relies on computer systems, networks, database servers, Microsoft Office 365, Sage ERP Payroll, and their website to conduct their business in Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane. These plans will help in protecting the company’s sensitive information, recover IT systems after disruption, and reduce employee downtime. A good contingency plan should provide procedures and technical measures for recovering IT systems after an unexpected event occurred (Swanson et al.,2010).

Backups

1.1 Hardware Failure Recovery Plan

Hardware failure may occur when an employee's desktop or laptop computer, printer, network equipment, or server stops working, reducing productivity.
Recovery actions:
• Employees must report the hardware failure to the Help Desk immediately.
• The Help Desk must record the incident and determine how serious the problem is.
• The IT Manager must identify whether the problem affects one employee, a department, or the entire branch.
• If an employee's computer fails, IT should provide a replacement computer for the employee to perform necessary tasks.
• Important business files must be restored from the company's backup storage.
• If the printer fails, staff should temporarily use an alternative printer if available or send necessary documents to another branch for printing.
• If the database server fails, the IT Manager must activate the server's recovery procedure and restore the latest reliable backup.
• Faulty equipment must be repaired or replaced as soon as possible.
• All hardware failures must be documented after the service has been recovered.

1.2 Network Failure Recovery Plan
AFC uses both wired and wireless network connections at its Cape Town, Durban, Polokwane, and Gqeberha branches. Network failure could prevent employees from accessing shared resources, the database, internet services, and other systems (West, 2024).
Recovery actions:
• The employees must report loss of network connectivity to the Help Desk.
• The Help Desk must determine whether failure affects one computer or the entire branch.
• IT team must check network cables, switches, wireless access points, and other network equipment.
• IT team must check whether internet service is available.
• If the Cape Town network fails, IT should determine whether critical work can temporarily be performed using available services from the Durban branch.
• If the Durban network fails, the affected employees should use alternative communication methods until the network is restored.
• Important business activities should be prioritized over non-essential network usage.
• The IT Manager must contact the internet service provider when the problem cannot be resolved internally.
• Employees must be informed when the network is expected to be restored.
• The incident must be recorded and reviewed after recovery.

1.3 Database Server Failure Recovery Plan
The database server is important because AFC uses IT systems to support its business operations. A database failure could affect access to important company information (Swanson et al., 2010).
Recovery actions:
• The IT Manager must immediately identify the cause of the database failure.
• Users must be prevented from making changes to the affected system until the database is confirmed to be safe.
• IT must restore the database using the most recent verified backup.
• The restored database must be checked whether it is complete and accurate.
• IT must confirm that authorized employees can access the database.
• If the main server cannot be recovered quickly, AFC should switch to another server to continue working while the main server is being fixed, there it is important for AFC to have an alternative server.
• Database recovery must be documented.
• Regular backup testing must be performed to ensure that backups can be used during an emergency.

1.4 Cybersecurity Incident Recovery Plan
AFC could experience malware, ransomware, phishing, unauthorized access or cyberattacks. Such an incident could affect computers, the database and company information, therefore the recovery actions that will guarantee business continuity during a cybersecurity incident are listed below (ISO, 2022).
Recovery actions:
• Employees must report suspicious emails, files, links, or unusual computer behavior immediately.
• The affected computer should be disconnected from the network to prevent the problem from spreading across the network.
• The IT Manager must thoroughly investigate the incident.
• Compromised user accounts must be temporarily disabled.
• IT must identify which systems and information have been affected.
• The affected systems must be cleaned, repaired, or rebuilt to ensure they are safe to use again.
• Data should be restored from a clean and verified backup.
• Passwords must be changed where accounts may have been compromised.
• Security updates and antivirus protection must be applied before affected devices are used again.
• Employees must be informed about the incident and provided training on malicious activities.
• The incident must be documented and reviewed to prevent it from happening again.

1.5 Power Failure and Physical Disaster Recovery Plan
Power failures, fire, flooding, theft, or other physical disasters could prevent staff from accessing AFC's IT equipment and workplace. Swanson.et al (2010) recommends the following recovery actions to promote business continuity during power failure or disaster.
Recovery actions:
• Staff must follow the company's safety procedures and vacate the building if necessary.
• IT equipment must only be accessed when the area has been declared safe.
• The IT Manager must assess the damage to computers, network equipment, printers, and servers.
• Important systems should be recovered using backup equipment or by working from an alternative location where possible.
• If all four office branches become unavailable, affected employees may temporarily work from home.
• Important business information should be recovered from secure backups stored onsite or offsite.
• Damaged equipment must be repaired or replaced.
• The company must communicate with employees about working arrangements.
• Normal operations should only resume after the building, equipment and network have been confirmed safe.

1.6 Data Loss Recovery Plan
Data could be lost because of accidental deletion, hardware failure, network failure malware, or other incidents (West, 2024).
Recovery actions:
• Employees must report missing or accidentally deleted information immediately.
• IT must determine what information has been lost.
• The IT Manager must identify the most recent reliable backup.
• Data must be restored to an approved system.
• Restored information must be checked before employees use it.
• Important company information should be backed up regularly.
• Backups should be protected from unauthorized access.
• At least one backup should be kept separately from the main system so that a single disaster does not destroy both the original information and its backup.
• Backup restoration should be tested regularly.

1.8 Health and Safety Emergency Recovery Plan
In the event of a pandemic, such as COVID-19, or national shutdown, riots, etc.
African Foods Company will be short staffed due to the rules and regulations consisting of social distancing and a minimum number of employees that can occupy a building to ensure that the virus does not spread rapidly (World Health Organization, 2020).
In case of a national shutdown or riots, employees are advised to stay and work from home to ensure their safety.
Therefore, the company should allow its employees to work from home, provide secure remote access to company systems, and encourage employees to follow relevant health and safety guidelines (Souppaya & Scarfone, 2016).
These measures will ensure that the business operations continue across all branches and disruptions will be minimized.

Testing

1.7 Disaster Recovery Testing
Swanson.et al (2021) states that AFC should not wait for a real disaster before testing its recovery plan because they need to be sure that what they have prepared will work when required.
The IT Manager should:
• Test backups regularly.
• Test restoration of the database.
• Test recovery of network services.
• Test replacement of failed computers.
• Test communication between the Cape Town, Durban, Polokwane, and Gqeberha branches.
• Conduct simulated cyberattack exercises.
• Review whether employees understand their responsibilities.
• Record problems were discovered during testing.
• Update the Disaster Recovery Plan when weaknesses are identified.
